AI today

AI is unmatched for boilerplate and syntax lookups, but frequently hallucinates subtle logical flaws that make debugging twice as tedious. It speeds up typing without genuinely understanding architecture or context. It works best as an eager junior developer whose every commit needs thorough code review.

5–10 years ahead

The job will shift from typing code to specifying requirements, edge cases, and architectural constraints. Autonomous agents may handle boilerplate; engineering value will center on verification, fault tolerance, and knowing what to build.

Hot take

“AI doesn’t make bad developers good; it just helps them generate catastrophic technical debt at record speed.”

AI today

AI assistants multiply productivity for well-understood domains and boilerplate, but break down in complex legacy codebases. They can be confidently wrong about synchronization and concurrency. AI is a fast copilot, but needs senior oversight.

Our shared perspective

The Future of Software Engineering

We see AI as a powerful change to how software gets made—not a substitute for engineering judgment. The work ahead is about giving automation the right constraints, checking what it produces, and keeping people at the center of the systems we build.

From typing to intent

As agents take on routine implementation, engineers can focus on requirements, edge cases, architecture, and meaningful outcomes.

Verification is the craft

Generated code needs careful review. Reliability, concurrency, security, privacy, and fault tolerance require people who can test assumptions.

Human context matters

Accessibility, thoughtful interfaces, domain understanding, and empathy help make systems useful to real people.

Use AI to move faster. Keep human judgment in charge of where you’re going.
